NVIDIA CORPORATION (NVDA) - Q3 FISCAL 2025 EARNINGS CONFERENCE CALL
Date: November 20, 2024
Executives:
- Jensen Huang, Founder and Chief Executive Officer
- Colette Kress, Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer

FINANCIAL HIGHLIGHTS & PREPARED REMARKS:
Colette Kress:
"Good afternoon, everyone. NVIDIA reported record revenue for the third quarter of fiscal 2025 of $35.1 billion, up 17% sequentially and up 94% from a year ago.
Data Center revenue was a record $30.8 billion, up 17% sequentially and up 112% year-on-year. Growth was driven by unprecedented demand for the NVIDIA Hopper computing platform, including H100 and H200 systems, as well as early shipments of our Blackwell architecture.
Networking revenue was $3.12 billion, up 20% sequentially, powered by Quantum InfiniBand and our new Spectrum-X Ethernet platform tailored for generative AI clusters.
Gaming revenue was $3.3 billion, up 15% year-on-year, driven by GeForce RTX 40 Series GPUs.
Professional Visualization revenue was $486 million, up 17% year-on-year.
Automotive revenue reached a record $449 million, up 72% year-on-year, driven by self-driving platforms and robotics partnerships.

GAAP gross margin for the quarter was 74.6%, and non-GAAP gross margin was 75.1%.
Operating expenses were $4.29 billion GAAP and $3.05 billion non-GAAP.
Net income reached $19.3 billion GAAP, representing a diluted EPS of $0.78. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $0.81.
Cash and cash equivalents, including marketable securities, stood at $38.5 billion.

OUTLOOK & GUIDANCE FOR Q4 FISCAL 2025:
Revenue is expected to be $37.5 billion, plus or minus 2%.
GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins are expected to be 73.0% and 73.5%, respectively, plus or minus 50 basis points.
We anticipate Blackwell production shipments to begin in Q4 and ramp into fiscal 2026. Because Blackwell requires new packaging techniques and initial ramp costs, gross margins in Q4 will see a temporary slight dip into the low 70s before recovering to the mid-70s later in fiscal 2026.
Full-year operating expenses are expected to grow in the mid-40% range.
Capital expenditures for Q3 were approximately $1.1 billion.

EXECUTIVE Q&A SESSION:
Analyst (Toshiya Hari, Goldman Sachs): "Jensen, can you address the demand trajectory and supply constraints for Blackwell? There have been rumors regarding overheating and engineering redesigns in NVL72 racks."
Jensen Huang: "Blackwell is in full production. We delivered our first customer samples in Q3, and we will ship more Blackwells this quarter than previously estimated. Demand for Blackwell is staggering; cloud service providers, sovereign AI nations, and enterprise hyperscalers are racing to build out infrastructure. Regarding server rack thermal design: Blackwell is a 208-billion transistor dual-die chip pulling up to 1,200 watts. We have engineered liquid cooling solutions across 100+ ecosystem partners. There are no fundamental thermal defects in our silicon or packaging. Every Blackwell chip we make over the next several quarters is completely sold out."

Analyst (Stacy Rasgon, Bernstein): "Colette, on gross margins, why the guidance decline to 73.5% in Q4, and when do we see margins returning to 75%?"
Colette Kress: "Stacy, as we transition from mature Hopper yields to brand-new Blackwell yields with complex CoWoS-L packaging, ramp-up scrap and component costs naturally press on margins. Once Blackwell reaches high-volume production in the second half of fiscal 2026, we expect gross margins to stabilize back into the mid-75% range."
